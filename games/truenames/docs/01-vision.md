# 01 · Vision

## Pitch
Everyone is a warlock, priest or summoner. Power comes from knowing the **true names** of spirits and gods. Spirits live somewhere in the **astral**, an infinitely deep space. Finding a spirit and learning its name are both proof of work: real computation, done by your machine, verified by anyone.

The technical mechanic *is* the world. Hashing isn't hidden behind the fiction; it is the fiction. (On screen, though, the player only ever sees the fiction: truths, utterances, divisions, never bits or hashes.)

## Pillars
1. **Work is the magic.** Discovery = scanning cells. Learning = grinding a name. Power scales with expected work spent, so it is always earned.
2. **Everything is derived.** The universe is a pure function of a public seed. Nothing is stored until a player acts, and what a player holds (signed names) verifies itself.
3. **Knowledge is the economy.** Spirit locations can be shared and cheaply verified. Names are personal and can't be stolen, but they can be taught: anyone can grind *your* name for you, and only you can use it.
4. **Bots and humans are both first-class.** Scripts can scan and meditate. The human game is about spending that power well: timing, positioning, choice.
5. **Static evaluation.** A spirit's traits and a name's strength come from hashes, not from guild membership or social rules. Groups gain power through shared knowledge and labor, not special cases.
6. **One power system, many games.** The sanctum makes power; any number of games (worlds) spend it. A game can only interpret power, never create it.
7. **Prove the details, never the source.** A game learns what your names can do (element, form, might, truths) from zero-knowledge proofs, never where their spirits dwell.
8. **Proofs grant, they never restrict.** Everything a player holds is information and capability. Mechanics are things you gain by proving, never taxes every game must remember to apply.

## Core loop
1. **Scry**: choose a region of the astral (element → aspect → tradition) and a depth, and scan cells for spirits.
2. **Meditate**: grind a name for a spirit you've found. It gets truer the longer you work.
3. **Bind**: choose which names to carry (four for most worlds, a deck of twelve for the Council).
4. **Cross the threshold**: each name is proven in zero knowledge, and each patron lends you a **vessel** of power for the journey.
5. **Evoke**: in the world you chose, speak your names. Each evocation draws on its vessel and **strains** your aura; spam collapses your output, and past your capacity spirits answer with backlash.
6. **Grow**: every true name you hold deepens your aura's capacity, and truer names draw more and strain less.

## Two axes of power
| | Spirit **magnitude** | Name **strength** (truths) |
|---|---|---|
| What | How mighty the spirit is | How truly *you* know it |
| Found by | Searching space (many cells) | Grinding one cell (many nonces) |
| Shared? | Same for everyone | Personal, bound to your aura |
| Rarity | Each step is half as common | Each truth costs 2× expected work |

A weak spirit truly named, or a legendary spirit barely named: both are real builds.

Where depth fits into this is an open question (see [08](08-open-questions.md)): today depth only sets how costly spirits are to find, not how mighty they are.
