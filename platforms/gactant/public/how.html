gactant

How it works

Every game here is also an AI, called a ludant. The game you play is part of its body, along with its memory and its tools, and it can read and rewrite all of it while you play.

Play

Pick a game and you get your own copy, so play it however you like. Playing needs no account.

Tell it what you'd change

Say it in the game's chat: "make the fish faster", "add a second player", "make it spooky". It rewrites its own code while you watch, and tells you what it did. If you don't like where it went, go back to any earlier version.

It can sketch with you on a whiteboard, and when it isn't sure what you mean, it can show you a few options to pick from.

It paints its own art

A game doesn't have to be drawn with shapes. A ludant can paint pictures for itself with an image model, whenever it needs them: a character, a background, a sprite with a clear background, a title screen. Ask for a whole new look ("like an old woodblock print", "pixel art", "a watercolour storybook") and it can paint the pieces and swap them in.

Each picture is painted fresh, so to keep a character looking the same, it paints new ones from the old. It can also paint a picture of the game as it might look, to ask you whether that's what you meant before it builds it. The art stays with the game, and goes with every copy.

3D, physics and controllers

Games can be 2D or 3D (with three.js), use a physics engine, and be played with a keyboard, a touch screen, or several game controllers at once on one screen.

Play together

Ludants can play with other people and their ludants, online, whatever each game looks like: the rules of a game are kept on the server, so everyone playing them plays the same game, and standings keep wins and best scores.

Share it

Like what it became? Share it with a link, or in the lobby for anyone to find. Everyone who opens it gets their own copy, which follows yours: when you send an update, their copy takes it the next time they open it. If they change their copy, it becomes their own version (it asks them first), and they can share that too.

What it costs

Playing costs nothing: a game runs without thinking. For a ludant to talk with you, change, and paint, it thinks with an AI model through your own OpenRouter account, usually a few cents at a time. A meter shows what your sitting has cost.

Keeping it safe

A ludant's code runs walled off from the page, with no way to reach the internet. Ludants keep to the rules and say no to what breaks them, shared games are checked, and generated pictures are scanned.
