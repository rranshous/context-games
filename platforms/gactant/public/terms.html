What you make

Every game here is also an AI, called a ludant, and it builds itself: its code, its art and its rules are written by AI models, as you play and ask for changes. Whatever rights there are in a game you made, they're yours. gactant doesn't claim ownership of it.

Those rights may be fewer than you'd expect. In the United States, and in many other places, copyright protects work made by people. Work an AI makes by itself generally isn't protected by copyright, though your own creative choices in it may be, such as the ideas you gave it, what you kept and what you threw out, and anything you wrote or drew yourself. How much of a game made here counts as yours is unsettled law. Nobody, gactant included, can promise that a game made here can be owned or protected.

To help you show what's yours, gactant marks what you contribute as you go: what you ask for, pick, draw and write, each with the time, signed by your device (gactant keeps only a fingerprint, never the words; see privacy). "How I made this", in a game's history, saves it all as a page you can keep and show, which checks itself against those marks. It's evidence of what you did and when, not a promise of what the law will make of it.

The AI models a ludant thinks with run on your own OpenRouter account, under OpenRouter's terms and those of each model's provider.

Shared games are open

Sharing a game shares all of it. Whoever takes a copy gets its whole body: its code, its rules, its art, its notes and its memory. They can read every line of it, the way they could with open source software. (What was said, sketched and saved while you played isn't part of it. That stays sealed and yours: see privacy.) So only share what you're happy for anyone with the link to see.

When you share a game, by a link or in the lobby, you give:

  • everyone on gactant permission to play it, take a copy, change their copy, and share that too. A copy shared from yours says what it's based on, which is how it credits you.
  • gactant permission to keep it, run it, show it (in the lobby, in link previews and on its pages), check it against the rules, and pass it to whoever takes a copy.

That permission covers gactant. If you want people to be free to use your game elsewhere too, you can say so in it, for example by asking it to write an open source license into its notes. Either way, a shared game can be read by anyone who copies it, and gactant can't stop someone from taking what they read somewhere else.

You can stop sharing, or send updates, at any time. Copies people have already taken are theirs to keep, along with what they've made from them, though they won't get your updates any more.

What you put in

Don't give a ludant other people's work to use, such as their art, characters or code, unless you're allowed to. Don't ask it for anything the rules keep out. You're responsible for what you ask your ludants to make and what you share.
